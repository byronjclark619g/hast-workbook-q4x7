The Brief (read this first)

You bought a business. Now what?

Here is the most important thing to know on day one: you are not the expert yet. Gus ran this business for 17 years. Denise has run the office for 6. Ray has been on a mower for 9. The fastest way for a new owner to wreck a good business is to walk in on day one and start changing things they don't understand yet. New owners who succeed do it in this order: learn first, write things down, change things later. Your first job is not to be the boss. Your first job is to figure out what you really bought.

The Business Model Map. Every business — a lawn company, a nail salon, a candy shop — can be drawn as one simple map with six boxes:

1. Customers — who pays us?
2. What we sell — what do they pay us for?
3. How the money comes in — one-time jobs? weekly? contracts?
4. What it costs to run — the big expense categories.
5. What we need to operate — people, trucks, equipment, a shop.
6. Why they pick us — what keeps customers here instead of a competitor?

If you can fill in those six boxes with real facts, you understand the business better than most people who work in one. If you can't fill in a box, that's not a failure — that's a question to go answer. Owners live in those six boxes all year.

One money word, again and forever: Profit = Revenue − Expenses. Revenue is all the money that comes in. Expenses are what it costs to run. Profit is what's left — it's what pays you, and it's what pays Gus his monthly payment. Notice that your case file's money table shows revenue and expenses… but never says the profit. That's your job.
