The Situation

Gus Kowalski started Calumet Lawn & Snow in Hammond in 2009 with one truck and one mower. Seventeen years later he is 64, his knees are shot, and his kids don't want the business. You just bought it. You used seller financing — Gus agreed to be paid over time out of the business's own profits. (You'll learn exactly how that kind of deal works later; some of you will learn it in New Venture Development. For now, one number matters: you owe Gus $2,600 per month. Keep that in the back of your mind — we will use it later in the year.)

Gus handed you the keys, a folder of paperwork, and a sticky note that says "Good luck kid. Call Denise if you get lost."

Everything below is what was in the folder.

1. Business Overview

  • What we do: Weekly lawn mowing (homes and businesses), landscaping projects (patios, mulch, planting), and snow plowing in winter.
  • Where: Hammond and nearby towns. The shop is a rented garage and yard.
  • Season: Mowing runs roughly April–October. Landscaping projects cluster May–September. Snow runs December–March, and only when it snows.
  • Founded: 2009. Same phone number the whole time. A lot of customers know Gus by name.

3. The Customers

  • 168 residential mowing accounts, average $38 per cut, weekly in season. No contracts — handshake deals. Customers can quit anytime, and a few do every year.
  • 9 commercial accounts (offices, a church, two apartment buildings). These ARE on contracts, renewed yearly.
  • Largest customer: Riverbend Office Park — mowing, landscaping, and snow. Riverbend alone is about 22% of all revenue. Gus's note: "Riverbend pays slow — 60 days sometimes. But it's a lot of money, so I never pushed."
  • 41 seasonal snow contracts (flat fee for the season, plus per-push billing on the big lots).

6. Gus's "Things I Never Got Around To" List (found in the folder)

  1. Prices haven't changed since 2022. "People love a deal. Also I hated that phone call."
  2. Nothing is written down. How we winterize the mowers, how Denise schedules, what to say when a customer complains — it's all in people's heads.
  3. Marcus is late about once a week. "I yelled. It worked for a few days."
  4. Riverbend pays in 60 days. Everyone else pays in about 2 weeks.
  5. We are not on Google. No reviews anywhere. "Never needed it. Not sure that's still true."
  6. Residential customers have no contracts. Every spring some just don't call back.
  7. Stan at Great Lakes Lawn two towns over "keeps talking about retiring. Somebody's going to get his 90 customers."

7. The Deal Note (file and remember)

You bought the business from Gus with seller financing: $2,600/month for 5 years. This payment is NOT in the expense table above (it's a payment on the purchase, not a cost of running the business). It comes out of profit. We will come back to this number in the money modules — and again at the end of the year, when you sell.
