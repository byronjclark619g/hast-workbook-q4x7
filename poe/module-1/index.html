The Brief (read this first)

There are two ways to get money.

Way 1: Earn it with your time. You work hours. Someone pays you for those hours. This is a wage (paid by the hour) or a salary (paid by the year). Most people earn money this way. It works — but it has a ceiling. There are only 24 hours in a day. If you stop working, the money stops too.

Way 2: Own something that earns. Some things you can own are able to make money for you. Those things are called assets. A car wash is an asset. A lawn-care company is an asset. A vending machine is an asset. When you own a business, your share of it is called equity. The business earns money whether or not you personally worked every hour — because the business has customers, workers, and a system.

Three money words you will use all year:

Revenue = all the money a business takes in.
Expenses = all the money a business spends to run.
Profit = what is left. Profit = Revenue − Expenses. Memorize this. You will use it more than any other formula in this class.

A true story. Wayne Huizenga was a college dropout who started out hauling garbage. In the 1960s he began with one garbage truck and one route of customers in Florida. He did not invent anything. Instead, he bought other small garbage routes, one after another, and combined them. That company became Waste Management, one of the biggest trash companies in the world. Later he did the same thing with video stores — buying and combining until he built Blockbuster Video, which sold in 1994 for about $8.4 billion. He also owned three professional sports teams. One man, no college degree, no invention — just buying and building ordinary businesses that earned money every day.

One more thing. Look around your town. The person who owns the plumbing company, the laundromat, or the taco shop may never look rich. Many quietly earn more than doctors — because they own assets, not just jobs. This class is about learning to see what they see.
